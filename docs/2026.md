# A note, January 2026

I wrote the first version of this on a Thursday afternoon in 2013. It was
four lines long and it told you a joke.

I have spent the last week reading what it became. I want to say clearly
that I don't think anyone did anything wrong. Every commit in this history
is a reasonable response to the commit before it.

The provider interface was reasonable: we genuinely thought we'd have a
second source of jokes. The XML was in a contract that paid our salaries
for two years. Splitting joke-service from laugh-service made both of them
easier to test. The consent banner is legally required. The Helm chart
replaced four hundred lines of YAML that we were copy-pasting by hand. The
model was how we learned to train models. The agents were how we learned to
orchestrate agents.

None of it was stupid. All of it was load-bearing for something.

But INCIDENT-4471 asked a question nobody wanted to own, so it sat in the
action items unassigned for a year, and I think it is the only question
here that ever mattered:

> Is the joke funny?

It is not. It stopped being funny around 2014. Everything built after that
point was built on top of a joke that had stopped working, and none of it
could have told us, because none of it was measuring that. We had a
readiness probe called `/funny` that returned 200 unconditionally. We had
nine hours of silence and six engineers looking at green dashboards.

So I am deleting it. Not because it is bad engineering -- most of it is
better engineering than I could do -- but because it is an extremely
sophisticated delivery mechanism for something nobody wants delivered.

The chain contract will outlive all of this. It is immutable. Somewhere
out there, forever, at a cost of four hundred and ten dollars, is a
chicken crossing a road.

Start again. Tell a better joke.

-- Priya
